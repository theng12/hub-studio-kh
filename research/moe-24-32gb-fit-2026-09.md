# Large/MoE open-weight LLMs that fit 24 GB / 32 GB Apple Silicon (late Sep 2026)

Target machines: **24 GB M4** (mid tier) and **32 GB M1 Max Mac Studio** (ceiling).
All on-disk GB below are **measured** by summing weight files from the Hugging Face API
(`/api/models/<repo>?blobs=true`) unless the cell says otherwise. "Peak mem" is the
**measured** unified-memory peak only where a source exists; otherwise inference.
Evidence: **M** measured (independent) · **V** vendor/quantizer self-test · **E** inference/estimate · **U** unverified.

## Notes

- **The single most important finding for the 24 GB tier:** the two ~30B-A3B Mamba/MoE
  families (Nemotron-3-Nano / 3.5-Lightning / Cascade-2, and GLM-4.7-Flash) land at
  **16.8–18.1 GB at MLX 4-bit**, which is the practical 24 GB sweet spot. The oMLX M4
  10-core 32 GB run of Nemotron Nano 4-bit peaked at **18.3 GB at 32k context** and
  **21.0 GB at 195k**, i.e. the hybrid Mamba-2 stack has an almost flat KV curve
  (only the 6 attention layers grow). The owner's 24 GB M4 is the same chip/GPU as that
  benchmark machine with 8 GB less RAM, so speed transfers and the usable context is
  roughly **32k comfortable / 64k tight**.
- **Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B GGUF is unusually large.** Every independent GGUF quantizer
  (ggml-org, unsloth, bartowski, mradermacher imatrix, mudler APEX) floors at
  **~18 GB even at IQ1_S/IQ2_XXS**, versus 9.9 GB for the third-party MLX 2-bit build.
  The cause is not documented here (**inference**: llama.cpp leaves a large fraction of
  the Mamba/MoE tensors at higher precision); the file sizes themselves are measured.
  Consequence: on 24 GB the only genuinely small Nemotron Nano option is the
  `majentik` MLX 2-bit repo (9.9 GB, quality unverified, single low-download uploader).
  For GGUF, treat ~17.9 GB as the floor and prefer GLM-4.7-Flash (10.5 GB GGUF IQ2_XXS)
  if a small GGUF is required.
- **Nemotron-3.5-Lightning (Aug 2026) is the newer, faster sibling** of Nano: same
  30 B/3 B shape, adds MTP/speculative decoding. Measured MLX 4-bit decode on an
  M3 Ultra is 168 t/s at 17.95 GB peak (quantizer's own test, so vendor-class). The
  M4 Pro 20c oQ8e 8-bit run (46.8 t/s @64k, 36.3 GB peak) shows the 8-bit variant is
  a 64 GB-machine proposition only.
- **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is the 32 GB M1 Max pick, not a 24 GB pick.** MLX 4-bit weights
  alone are 20.4 GB and a measured M5 Max peaked at 25.1 GB @64k / 31.8 GB @128k.
  Its Gated-DeltaNet hybrid keeps KV small, so on 32 GB ~64k is realistic. On 24 GB,
  use the GGUF Q3_K_M (16.6 GB) / IQ4_XS (17.7 GB) instead, or Qwen3.8-27B dense MLX
  4-bit (16.1 GB).
- **Gemma-4-26B-A4B QAT is the best "fits everywhere" multimodal option**: 15.6 GB MLX
  4-bit, measured 17.6 GB peak at 64k on an M4 32 GB with 34.8 t/s @1k. It has a vision
  tower (~550 M), relevant to the app's single VLM call site. Google also ships official
  QAT q4_0 GGUF, which is a rare quality guarantee at 4-bit.
- **Nemotron Nano-Omni is the only Nemotron with vision**, but its 4-bit MLX weight set is
  19.6 GB and GGUF Q4_K_M is 24.5 GB + 1.6 GB mmproj, so it is a 32 GB-only, short-context
  option.
- **Nemotron-4**: no MoE variant is a fit. Nemotron-4 340B and Llama-3.1-Nemotron-70B are
  far outside 32 GB; the useful descendants are the Nemotron-3 family above.
- **Dense 27–32 B models (Qwen3.8-27B, Gemma-4-31B, Granite 4.2-30B, Olmo-3.1-32B)**
  are all 16.1–18.4 GB at MLX 4-bit, so they fit 32 GB comfortably and 24 GB only at
  ~16–32k context. They are slower per token than the 3 B-active MoEs.
- Speeds measured on machines **other than the owner's** are labelled with the exact
  machine in the table. Only the M4 10-core rows are the owner's mid-tier chip (32 GB
  variant); no published measurement on a **24 GB** M4 or on a **32 GB** M1 Max was
  found — those two cells are explicitly inference.
