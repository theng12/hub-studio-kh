# MoE vs dense text models for the fleet — is MoE faster? (2026-09)

Date: 2026-09-26. Status: **research only — nothing adopted or qualified.** Owner direction
recorded in [`research/moe-fleet-qualification-handoff.md`](moe-fleet-qualification-handoff.md)
(Granite 4.0 H-Tiny 4-bit MLX for 8 GB, LFM2.5-8B-A1B 4-bit MLX for 16 GB, dense Qwen3.5-9B
4-bit kept for scripts) — a qualification plan, not an adopted decision. Evidence class is
labelled per claim: **[M]** measured, **[V]** vendor claim, **[I]** inference.
Every model is named with its quantization. Fleet per `research/owner-hardware.md`.

## The question

The current common model, **Qwen3.5-9B 4-bit** (dense), runs but is slow: owner-measured
**12 tok/s on M1, 14 on M2, 18 on M4 16 GB** [M, owner 2026-09-26]. Would a **Mixture-of-Experts
(MoE)** model be faster?

## Short answer

**Yes — but only inside the same memory budget, and on 16 GB that means the *small* MoEs, not the
famous 30B-A3B ones.**

MoE does not reduce *memory*: for single-device inference every expert stays resident, so peak RAM
tracks **total** parameters. What it reduces is **bytes read and FLOPs per generated token**,
because only the **active** experts run. Speed therefore scales with *active* params, while
**fit still scales with total params**. On a 16 GB Mac the binding constraint is total size, so a
35B-A3B model is simultaneously "fast per token" and "will not fit."

## Why the dense 9B is slow on a base chip

A dense 9B 4-bit model reads almost all ~6 GB of weights for every token. Base M1/M2/M4 have
memory bandwidth roughly an order of magnitude below a Max/Ultra part, which is exactly the
12–18 tok/s the owner sees [M, owner]. A 1–1.5B-active MoE reads only ~0.7–1.2 GB per token
[I], so the same machine should generate several times faster — the win is real, but it is a
function of *active* params, not of the "MoE" label.

## Update 2026-09-27 — measured on the actual 16 GB tier

Two independent 16 GB Mac mini builds now exist and both land on the same design: **a 3B-active MoE
compressed to ~10 GB, with 2-bit routed experts and 3-bit attention.** This is the first
fleet-tier measurement of the MoE speedup, and it confirms the inference above.

### The whole argument is one memory budget

A 16 GB Mac mini does not have 16 GB to spend [M, measured budget on an M4 mini]:

| | GB |
| --- | ---: |
| Total unified memory | 16.0 |
| macOS + background services | −3.5 |
| Model, peak | −11.3 |
| **Left for KV cache** | **1.2** |

So **~11 GB of weights is the practical ceiling**, and it is tight. Everything at 12.5 GB and up
cannot hold a usable context, no matter how good it looks on a spec sheet.

Three things fall out:

1. **MoE does deliver, and the win is ~2–4×, not the ~10× the parameter ratio suggests.** 61 and 30
   tok/s against 16–20 tok/s for the dense 9B on the same machine.
2. **Out-of-core streaming is a dead end.** Once the model stops fitting, speed collapses to
   0.18–5.4 tok/s regardless of architecture. **The model must sit in RAM.** MoE still beats dense
   under streaming (5.4 vs 0.18 tok/s — only the ~8 active experts of 256 are read, ~14 MB per
   layer instead of the whole FFN) but 5.4 tok/s is not a writing speed.
3. **On bytes-per-token, the two best options are the same speed.** LFM2.5-8B-A1B at 4-bit reads
   ~1.5B × 4 bits ≈ 0.75 GB/token; Qwen3.5-35B-A3B at 2.58 bpw reads ~3B × 2.58 bits ≈ 0.97
   GB/token. Nearly equal — so the choice between them is **not** a speed choice, it is a
   memory-squeeze and quantization-risk choice at equal throughput.

- **~1–1.5B active**: M1 **~45–53**, M2 **~66–78**, M4 **~85–103** tok/s — **3–4× the dense 9B**.
- **~3–4B active**: only `gpt-oss-20b` fits; M1 ~22–24, M2 ~33, M4 ~40 [I, scaled from the M2 rate].
- **30B-A3B class at 4-bit needs 24 GB**: M2 24 GB ~32, M3 Pro 36 GB ~38, M1 Ultra ~70 tok/s.

**Never mix harnesses in one table.** A separate controlled M4 mini 16 GB harness measured
**LFM2-8B-A1B MLX 4-bit at 38.9 tok/s against oMLX's 86.9 on the same class of chip** — a 2.2×
spread from the runner alone. oMLX's dense-9B figures (~19.6–19.7 on M2) sit near the owner's 14, so
oMLX is roughly calibrated to his baseline; that is the justification for leaning on it, and also
why the **61 tok/s for the 2.58 bpw Qwen3.5-35B-A3B** (a different publisher's MLX harness) must not
be dropped into the same column as these.

**Unresolved contradiction on Gemma 4 26B-A4B.** oMLX measures it running at ~41–42 tok/s on an M4

over a 128-expert `gather_mm` MLX bug. The two differ in engine, so the bug is likely engine- or
version-specific rather than chip-wide. **Re-test on the 24 GB M4 with the engine we actually ship**
before trusting either claim; this is not settled.

**Fit still binds before speed does.** Qwen3-30B-A3B 4-bit is 17.19 GB (does not fit 16 GB at all)
and Gemma 4 26B-A4B 4-bit is 15.64 GB (borderline, and only its ~3-bit form fits). On 16 GB only the
~4–5 GB small MoEs and the ~11 GB `gpt-oss-20b` / 2.58 bpw Qwen3.5-35B-A3B shapes fit at all.

### Why 2-bit experts do not collapse the model

Not every parameter is equally sensitive, and MoE makes the insensitive ones easy to find. The
published recipe [V] keeps everything that runs *every* token at high precision and compresses only
the sparse part:

| Component | Bits | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MoE router (`mlp.gate`) | **bf16** | quantizing it selects wrong experts → hallucination |
| Shared-expert gate, norms, GDN params | **bf16** | tiny and quality-critical |
| Full attention (10 of 40 layers) | 3-bit | active every token |
| GatedDeltaNet / linear attention (30 layers) | 3-bit | 2-bit here caused repetition loops |
| Shared expert | 3-bit | always active |
| **Routed experts** (40 × 256, top-8) | **2-bit** | only ~3.1% of pathways fire per token |

Cost of 3.50 → 2.58 bpw, lm-eval 0-shot [V]: average **64.25% → 61.34% (−2.90pp)**, worst hits
HellaSwag −4.35pp and Winogrande −6.16pp. The alternative is not "same size at 3-bit": uniform
3-bit is 14 GB and **does not fit 16 GB at all**.

Its KV cache is unusually cheap, which is what makes the squeeze survivable: only 10 of 40 layers
use full attention, GQA gives 2 KV heads, so 4-bit KV costs ~5 KB/token (0.62 GB at 128K) plus a
fixed ~33 MB GatedDeltaNet state [V].

### The catch list — this is a deliberately squeezed configuration

- **Thinking must be off.** With 1.2 GB of KV headroom, the default reasoning trace (2000+ tokens)
  exhausts memory and OOMs. `enable_thinking: false` is mandatory, not a preference.
- **Nothing else may be running.** Safari one tab ≈ 0.5 GB, Slack/Discord ≈ 0.3 GB; the publisher's
  own budget shows either one tipping it into a crash. Headless + SSH is recommended to reclaim
  ~0.5 GB from WindowServer.
- **Sampling is load-bearing**: `presence_penalty ≥ 1.5` (below it, repetition loops → runaway
  tokens → OOM), `temperature > 0` (greedy loops on quantized experts), `max_tokens ≤ 2048`.
- **Text-only.** The `mlx_lm` conversion drops the vision encoder; the VLM variant (~10.8 GB, peak
  12.1 GB) leaves only ~0.4 GB of KV — not usable on 16 GB.
- **Repetition is more likely at 2 bits**, even with correct sampling. That is the specific failure
  to test on long-form prose.

### What is *not* a 16 GB MoE, despite looking like one

- **Gemma 4 E4B is dense, not MoE.** A widely-copied "Mac Mini M4 16GB" benchmark table labels it
  "4B MoE"; the official Gemma 4 card describes E2B/E4B as **dense + PLE** (E4B = 4.5B effective,
  8B with embeddings), and MoE begins at `26B A4B` [M, model card]. Its 35 tok/s is a small-dense
  result and **must not be cited as MoE evidence**.
- **Gemma 4 26B-A4B is reported broken on M4 base under MLX.** The same publisher reports an MLX
  bug in which the 128-expert `gather_mm` Metal kernel malfunctions on M4 base (10 GPU cores);
  their Gemma 4 26B project "failed to deploy on M4 Mac Mini" and they switched to Qwen3.5, whose
  256 experts take a different MLX path [V, one publisher]. This directly affects the **24 GB M4
  fallback** in `moe-fleet-qualification-handoff.md` and must be re-tested there.
- **`baa-ai/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-RAM-16GB-MLX` is named for a size point, not for your machine.** It is
  **15.87 GB on disk** and its own card recommends **~21 GB** of unified memory. That collection
  starts at 12.5 GB; none of it fits a 16 GB Mac with usable context. Read the number, not the name.
- **Granite 4.1 has no MoE at all.** IBM's April 2026 refresh ships **dense** 3B / 8B / 30B and
  explicitly *returns to a dense transformer* after the 4.0 generation's hybrid Mamba/MoE
  experiment [M, IBM/Granite 4.1 write-up]. So Granite 4.1-8B is a **quality** answer (it matches
  the prior 32B MoE on IFEval, BFCL v3 68.27, MMLU 73.84) but **not a speed** answer — being dense,
  it lands in the same 12–20 tok/s band as the 9B. `granite-4.0-h-tiny` stays the family's only MoE
  and therefore our 8 GB candidate.
- **`Brooooooklyn/Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-UD-Q2_K_XL-mlx` is 13 GB** — 86.1 tok/s, but measured on an
  **M3 Max 128 GB** [V, publisher], and 13 GB + 3.5 GB macOS leaves nothing for KV. Too big by
  ~2 GB for this tier. (It does cross-validate the M4 number: ~86 tok/s at M3 Max bandwidth scales
  to ~34 tok/s at M4's, near the 30 tok/s measured independently.)

### MLX pitfall, verified in our own runtime

`nn.quantize()` **silently skips MoE experts**: in `mlx_lm`, `SwitchLinear` and
`QuantizedSwitchLinear` subclass `nn.Module`, *not* `nn.Linear` [M, inspected in
`.build/lance-runtime/…/mlx_lm/models/switch_layers.py`]. Experts left in float16 are both huge and
wrong once the rest of the model is quantized.

**`mlx_lm`'s own converter is safe** — `utils.py` builds its `class_predicate` from
`hasattr(m, "to_quantized")`, `SwitchLinear` defines `to_quantized`, and `QuantizedSwitchLinear` is
handled on the dequantize path [M, inspected]. The trap only catches hand-rolled quantization, which
is exactly what we would write if we ever converted a MoE ourselves.

**Revised 16 GB recommendation:** qualify **both** — `LFM2.5-8B-A1B 4-bit` (4.85 GB, comfortable,
4-bit quality, 1.5B active) as the safe default, and the **2.58 bpw Qwen3.5-35B-A3B** (10 GB, peak
11.3 GB, 3B active, ~35B capacity) as the capability option — at the *same* expected speed. Keep
the dense `Qwen3.5-9B 4-bit` as the prose control, since the 2-bit experts' effect on long-form
writing is unmeasured.

### Correction 2026-09-28 — LFM2.5 cannot have its thinking switched off

The small-MoE survey (`research/small-moe-16gb-2026-09.md`) plus a direct read of the shipped
template settles something the tier plan assumed. **`LiquidAI/LFM2.5-8B-A1B` is a thinking model and
its `chat_template.jinja` accepts no disable flag** [M, template read 2026-09-28]: the only thinking
knob is `preserve_thinking`, which controls whether *past* assistant traces are re-rendered into
history — it is not an off switch for generation. There is no `enable_thinking` variable at all.

That collides with our own worker. `components/chat/app/backend/llm_engine.py` passes
`enable_thinking=thinking` and falls back on `except TypeError` — but an extra, **unreferenced**
template variable raises nothing, so the fallback never fires and **thinking stays on, silently**.
The `<think>` trace then precedes the answer, breaks JSON mode and eats the token budget, with no
error to surface. There is no think-stripping anywhere in `components/chat/` [M, searched].

**The grammar lane saves it, by construction.** Under GGUF + JSON-schema/GBNF the sampler can only
emit grammar-valid tokens, so `<think>` is unreachable and validity holds regardless of the model's
reasoning habit. That sharpens the rule beyond "prefer grammar for JSON":

- **GGUF + grammar lane** — LFM2.5-8B-A1B is fine.
- **Native MLX lane** — do not put LFM2.5-8B-A1B on a `JSON.parse` call site until the worker strips
  everything up to and including `</think>`. That is a code change, not a prompt tweak.

`Qwen3.5-9B 4-bit` keeps its `enable_thinking` flag and is unaffected. **Granite 4.0 H-Tiny 4-bit**
is non-reasoning (no trace at all) and carries the best measured small-model JSON pedigree (SOB JSON
Pass 98.3%), so it stays the safe JSON choice at 8 GB and 16 GB alike.

**New entrant worth qualifying:** `nightmedia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-Labs-3-Elastic-12B-A2B-mxfp4-mlx` —
12B total / **2.0B** active, **6.63 GB** on disk, and it does support `enable_thinking=False` [V].
It is a community quant of an extracted slice of NVIDIA's 3-in-1 elastic 30B checkpoint, so it
carries the usual provenance caveat, and it needs the same reading. Its fit is comfortable where the
35B-A3B is squeezed, which makes it the interesting middle option for 16 GB.

## Quality — the real trade

- **LFM2.5-8B-A1B 4-bit** is the strongest small-MoE candidate: instruction-following and
  structured-output numbers well above Granite H-Tiny and near a dense 9B, and it is explicitly
  positioned for **structured outputs, tool use and on-device assistants** [V]. It is a
  **reasoning model** — it emits an explicit chain of thought, so the app must suppress or budget
  that exactly as it must for Qwen3.5's `Thinking Process` trace [V].
- **Granite 4.0 H-Tiny 4-bit** is the smallest/fastest and has the longest pedigree for document
  and JSON extraction, but its **creative-writing score is weak (32.60)** [V], which matters for
  long-form narration.
- **Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 4-bit** is the dark horse: dense, only **2.28 GB**, faster than the 9B,
  and its creative-writing signal (51.71) is *better than LFM2-8B-A1B's 44.22* in the same vendor
  table [V]. If prose quality is the constraint, this may beat the small MoEs.
- **Prose at 1–1.5B active is the open risk.** No independent long-form narration evaluation was
  found for LFM2.5-8B-A1B or Granite 4.0 H-Tiny 4-bit. The dense Qwen3.5-9B 4-bit remains the
  known-prose model; MoE is the *speed* bet, not yet a quality-proven one.

## Prose and JSON are different problems — weigh them separately

**JSON is an engine property, not a model property.** Grammar/schema-constrained decoding
(llama.cpp GBNF or a JSON schema) makes validity ~100% and is largely model-agnostic. The same
checkpoint in MLX and GGUF is the same weights and the same quality — GGUF is chosen for the JSON
lane only because llama.cpp can constrain decoding and the plain `mlx-lm` server cannot. Both
candidate MoEs are supported by llama.cpp — **LFM2-MoE hybrid**
([PR #16464](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/16464)) and **Granite hybrid**
(`LLM_ARCH_GRANITE_HYBRID`, `"granitehybrid"`) — so the outline arrays, structured image prompts
and metadata extraction can run on the fast MoE through **GGUF + grammar** with JSON risk off the
table. Native, unconstrained generation never reaches 100% (best measured Structured Output
Benchmark pass 0.987 [M]); the grammar is the fix, not a bigger model.

**Prose is a model property, and length is the stress test.** POLARIS
([arXiv 2606.04095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04095), EMNLP 2026) trains **Qwen3.5-9B** and opens
with the finding that small open-weight models *"either fall far short of the requested length, or
their quality significantly degrades as length increases … length generalization is a meaningful
stress test."* Creative-Writing-v3 signals for the candidates [V, Liquid AI comparison table]:
Gemma-3-4b-it **68.56**, Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 **51.71**, LFM2-8B-A1B **44.22**,
Granite-4.0-H-Tiny **32.60**. The small MoEs are the *weakest* prose options, and Granite H-Tiny
is explicitly a structured-extraction model, not a writer.

**Map each artifact you actually write to the axis that decides it:**

| Artifact | Shape | What decides quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Image prompts | descriptive, often JSON-ish | **grammar + speed** — a fast MoE is fine |
| Outline | JSON array | **grammar** — a fast MoE is fine |
| Metadata (title/description/tags) | short prose | **speed** — a fast MoE is fine |
| Script / narration | long-form prose | **model quality + length adherence** — keep the dense 9B |

`rishanthrajendhran/POLARIS-9B` is that same dense 9B fine-tuned with GRPO to write long stories
much better (competitive with Qwen3.5-27B per its blinded evaluation), but it is **gated**
(HF access approval required), **18.84 GB** bf16, with **no MLX quant found** — not a drop-in. It
does show the prose lever is *training*, not architecture.

## Recommendation — what to qualify, in order

The "one model across the fleet" rule is about not keeping a different model **per machine**; it
does not require one model for every **artifact**. Given prose and JSON pull in opposite
directions, qualify a two-lane setup and only collapse it if one model passes both:

1. **Speed + JSON lane** for image prompts, outline and metadata:
   **`LiquidAI/LFM2.5-8B-A1B-MLX-4bit` (4.85 GB)** at 16 GB, or
   **`mlx-community/granite-4.0-h-tiny-4bit` (3.92 GB)** where 8 GB fit matters — both served
   through **GGUF + JSON-schema grammar** for the `JSON.parse` sites.
2. **Prose lane** for the script: keep **Qwen3.5-9B 4-bit** (dense, known prose). Do not move the
   script to a 1–1.5B-active MoE without a measured long-form comparison — that is exactly where
   POLARIS says small models fail.
3. **Dense control to try**: **`mlx-community/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-4bit` (2.28 GB)** — faster
   than the 9B, and a *better* creative-writing signal than either small MoE (51.71 vs 44.22 /
   32.60). If it holds up on a real chapter, it may beat the MoE for a single model.
4. Keep **Qwen3.5-9B 4-bit** as the prose model and **Gemma 4 26B-A4B QAT 4-bit** /
   **Qwen3-30B-A3B 4-bit** as the 24 GB fallback for the two M4-24 machines.

If one model for everything is non-negotiable, the test is: give **LFM2.5-8B-A1B 4-bit** a full
long-form chapter prompt and judge **length adherence and quality at 8–12k characters** against
the dense 9B. If it degrades, it is a drafting and prompts model, not the script model.

Run every candidate on the **real artifacts** — outline (JSON), one chapter (prose), thumbnail
prompt set (JSON), metadata — and record tok/s, peak memory, **JSON validity** and a prose
verdict per machine. Do not choose on MMLU alone.

## Caveats

- MoE **speed is not free of routing overhead**; the real speedup is measured, not the 4× the
  parameter ratio suggests.
- Hybrid Mamba/MoE decode has known slow kernels in some runtimes (e.g. `mlxcel` issue #1685
  reports `GraniteMoeHybrid` at ~⅓ of `mlx-lm` speed) — throughput is runtime-dependent.
- Both LFM2.5 and Qwen3.5 emit reasoning traces by default; a thinking toggle is required either
  way.
- Quantization changes quality; the 3-bit tiers are for 8 GB fit, not for fidelity.
