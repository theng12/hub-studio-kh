When Mara first arrived in the village, the river was nearly silent, and the old mill stood still beside the water. Nobody could remember when its wheel had last turned. Every morning she walked the narrow path along the bank, carrying a basket of bread for the baker's widow, and every morning she paused to listen. On the fourth day, she heard something new: a soft knocking, like a patient hand on a wooden door. She followed the sound to the mill and found a young heron trapped between two rotting beams. Carefully, she freed it, and the bird rose into the pale light without a single cry. That evening, the villagers gathered at her door, curious and a little afraid. They had watched the heron circle the roof three times before it vanished over the hills. The baker's widow smiled and said that the mill always chose its friends. By spring, the wheel was turning again, and the river had found its voice, singing over the stones as though nothing had ever been lost.
